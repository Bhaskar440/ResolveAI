# Northwind Supply Co. — Dispute Resolution Guidelines

These guidelines tell the Resolution Agent how to weigh evidence, when to defend, and
when to ask for more information instead of guessing.

## Resolution principles

Resolve on verified transaction evidence and the merchant's published policies, never on
assumptions. The buyer's statement and the merchant's records must both be considered.
Prefer the least costly remedy that fully addresses the proven harm. Explain the decision
in plain language and cite the policy sections relied on. A decision must not invent
tracking events, delivery dates, photos, or refund records.

Tags: resolution, verified evidence, policies, least costly remedy, explanation, citations

## Evidence sufficiency

If the available evidence cannot establish the facts needed for a decision, request more
information instead of inventing facts. Missing information includes a transaction or
capture record, a reason code, the requested resolution, tracking or delivery proof, or
the policy section that governs the dispute. The request must name exactly what is
missing and why it matters. The agent may still explain the likely outcomes, but it must
not propose or execute a payment until the evidence is sufficient.

Tags: insufficient evidence, request more information, missing evidence, transaction, tracking, policy

## Buyer credibility and consistency

Weigh the buyer's account for internal consistency and consistency with the transaction
record. A buyer who provides a clear description, dates, and supporting photos is more
credible than one who contradicts the record. Do not deny a claim solely because the
buyer lacks a receipt when the merchant's own records show the payment.

Tags: credibility, consistency, buyer statement, photos, transaction record, receipt

## Merchant evidence

The merchant's order, capture, shipping, and refund records are authoritative for what
the merchant did. A merchant assertion without a record is weaker than a carrier scan or
a payment capture. If the merchant cannot produce a required record, say so and treat it
as an evidence gap, not as proof that the merchant is right.

Tags: merchant evidence, order record, capture, shipping, refund record, evidence gap

## Unauthorised payments and fraud

The merchant does not refund unauthorised-payment claims directly. These must be
handled through PayPal's dispute process with fraud review. The merchant may defend
with device, delivery and account-history evidence. No autonomous money movement is
allowed for unauthorised or duplicate-transaction reasons.

Tags: UNAUTHORISED, fraud, chargeback, duplicate transaction, defend, no autonomous payment

## Decision confidence

Confidence must reflect the strength and completeness of the evidence. High confidence
requires a verified capture, a clear policy match, and no material evidence gaps.
Medium confidence is appropriate when one material fact is missing but the policy outcome
is still clear. Low confidence requires escalation or a request for more information.
Never inflate confidence to unlock autonomous execution.

Tags: confidence, evidence strength, high confidence, medium confidence, low confidence, escalation

## Defending a dispute

Defend when the verified evidence shows the merchant fulfilled the order, the buyer
received the item as described, and the policy does not require a refund. A defence
must cite the delivery scan, the listing description, and the applicable policy. Do not
defend on the basis of the merchant's reputation or an unverified assertion.

Tags: defend, fulfilled, delivery scan, listing, policy, unverified assertion
